Figure: Comparative Architecture of ASR Post-Processing Correction Methods

Swahili offline ASR pipeline — visual-impaired-assistant (ai.pivotstudio.via.android)
Methodology. All methods were evaluated on the same 96-transcript set (30 Swahili Common Voice clips + 2 real on-device recordings, × 3 ASR engines each), scored for Word/Character Error Rate against identical ground-truth references, measured against a baseline mean WER of 0.6744 (uncorrected ASR output). LLM-based correction (AfriTeva v2 Base, Swahili Gemma 1B) consistently increased WER by hallucinating fluent but acoustically-unrelated text — a known failure mode in post-ASR correction literature for low-resource languages. Dictionary-based correction, bounded to a known Swahili vocabulary and therefore structurally incapable of hallucinating novel text, reduced WER when built on a morphology-aware lexicon (Hunspell sw_TZ, 71.5k words) and improved further (12:1 improve:worsen ratio, best result overall) when gated to abstain on ambiguous edit-distance ties rather than guessing. A full morphological/noun-class agreement checker (apertium-swa) was confirmed technically feasible fully offline with no superuser access, but its current noun lexicon coverage is insufficient for production use and is left as future work.
