# Comparison: The 4 Accessible-Transcription Swahili ASR Dataset Entries

What each paper/project actually contributes, and how they differ. Scope: only the 4 entries confirmed to have directly downloadable Swahili audio+transcription (see `training-data-access-verification.md`).

**Important correction first**: the `AfriSpeech/african-speech-public_v1` dataset (which we analyzed for phonetic balance) is **not** from the TACL "AfriSpeech-200" paper (arXiv:2310.00274, Intron Health) as previously stated — that paper covers **Pan-African accented English**, a different project entirely. The `AfriSpeech` HuggingFace org we actually used is an unrelated open-source data-curation initiative (afrispeech.org) that re-hosts existing religious-speech recordings (Open Bible Speech / Faith Comes By Hearing, LDS General Conference talks) across African languages. It has no academic paper behind it — it's a rehosting/curation project, not original research. This also explains the religious-content skew we measured (Yehova = 5th most frequent word): the source material literally is Bible and Church broadcast content.

## Comparison table

| Entry | Is it a research paper? | What it actually contributes | Data origin | Swahili accessible size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FLEURS (sw_ke)** | Yes — Conneau et al. 2022, Google Research, published at IEEE SLT | A new **evaluation benchmark**, not a training-focused release. Built on FLoRes-101 (machine-translation benchmark) sentences read aloud, parallel across 102 languages. Contribution is methodological: proves that ~12 hrs/language is enough to meaningfully evaluate (not train) low-resource ASR, speech-language-ID, translation and retrieval, using the same sentence set across every language for direct comparability. | Professional-style read speech of Wikipedia/FLoRes sentences | ~12 hrs total (3.62h train + 0.60h test, confirmed) |
| **w2v-bert-2.0-swahili-asr** | No — model card only, no paper | A **fine-tuned model release**, not research. Its real contribution is the underlying dataset it draws on: 400+ hrs of genuinely **multi-domain** (Health, Government, Finance, Education, Agriculture) human-transcribed Swahili, funded by Gates Foundation via Digital Umuganda/CLEAR Global. This is the only entry in our whole landscape table with deliberate domain diversity rather than a single genre (news, religious text, or scripted sentences). Base architecture (Wav2Vec2-BERT 2.0) itself comes from Meta's Seamless paper (not Swahili-specific; trained on 4.5M hrs/143 languages). | Real-world multi-domain transcribed speech | 400+ hrs (full set = full training corpus, confirmed) |
| **AfriSpeech (swahili_swa)** | No — not a paper, a data-curation/rehosting project (afrispeech.org) | **Not original research** — re-hosts existing religious speech corpora (Bible readings, LDS General Conference) for 140+ African languages under one unified HuggingFace schema, making them easier to load/filter than scattered originals. Its contribution is convenience/access, not new data collection or new science. | Re-hosted Bible/Church recordings | 38.73 hrs total (3.86 hrs test+val directly verified by us; religious-content skew confirmed) |
| **whisper-small-swh-finetuned** | No — model card only, no paper | A **fine-tuning demonstration**, not research. Shows that taking Whisper-small and fine-tuning on just 3.62 hours of FLEURS Swahili data produces a 68% relative WER reduction (103.10% → 32.07%). Its contribution is purely empirical/practical: proof that even a benchmark-sized dataset (not built for training) is enough to meaningfully improve a general multilingual model on Swahili specifically. Uses the same underlying data as FLEURS above — not an independent data source. | Same as FLEURS (reused) | Same as FLEURS (3.62h train) |

## What this means together

Only **one of the four** (FLEURS) is genuine peer-reviewed research with a stated scientific contribution (a reusable, parallel, multilingual few-shot evaluation benchmark). The other three are **practical releases, not papers**:
- w2v-bert-2.0-swahili-asr's real value is its **training data's domain diversity** — the only multi-domain Swahili corpus in our table, in contrast to AfriSpeech's single-genre religious skew.
- AfriSpeech is a **convenience rehosting layer**, not new research or new data collection — its scientific contribution is essentially zero; its practical contribution is making religious-speech Swahili data one `load_dataset()` call away.
- whisper-small-swh-finetuned is a **replication/demo**, reusing FLEURS's own data to show a fine-tuning result — not an independent dataset contribution at all.

**Practical implication for this app**: if we want genuinely diverse Swahili training/eval data (not a single narrow domain), `w2v-bert-2.0-swahili-asr`'s underlying 400hr corpus (`badrex/swahili-speech-400hr`) is the strongest candidate among these four — it's the only one with real multi-domain coverage and a fully open, unauthenticated download path.
